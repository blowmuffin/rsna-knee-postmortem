## Key Question 1: What do the late-Sep to Oct 2026 discussions reveal (new tricks, shared resources, host rulings, test-set composition)?

### Takeaway
The single biggest new technical item is the slice-order trap: sorting slices by DICOM filename (a random SOP UID) instead of physical position. It was reported again on 2026-10-04 with a measured +0.0276 gain on one fold, and our `knee.py` already sorts physically, so it does not apply to us. The host has still not answered the external-data, public-weight, RadImageNet-license or test-composition questions as of 2026-10-04. The only rulings on record are the August LLM-API/dataset-accessibility rulings and a 2026-09-21 OAI statement. Key dates: team-merge deadline 2026-10-15, final submission 2026-10-22.

### Cited Findings
**Technical**
- 2026-10-04, willyp74: the public baseline's `read_slot()` stacks slices with `sorted(filename)` (filenames are SOP Instance UIDs). On 60 random series the median |Spearman rho| between filename order and ImagePositionPatient order was 0.164 (0.0% of series above 0.9, 98.3% below 0.5; not reversed, just shuffled). One fold, same seed and folds, 12 epochs: filename order 0.7695 vs IPP order 0.7971 (+0.0276). A second seed on the patched version gave 0.7960 (seed noise about 0.001). Fix: sort by IPP along the through-plane axis, InstanceNumber as fallback; do it inside `read_slot` and cache per series (putting it in `probe()` read ~820k headers and added ~7 min). — [745759](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/rsna-knee-abnormality-detection/discussion/745759#3532620)
- Corroboration: stevenleehans, 2026-08-16: "sorted filename order matches true anatomical order only about 5% of the time"; sorts by IPP projected on the IOP normal with fallbacks SliceLocation, InstanceNumber, filename; crops a fixed physical extent in mm because FOV takes 71 distinct values (median 160 mm); on his CV 3 slices beat 9 by ~4x his noise floor (confounded with slice position). — [735154](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/rsna-knee-abnormality-detection/discussion/735154#3513374)
- Corroboration: gchauhan EDA, 2026-09-14: InstanceNumber runs against slice position in over a third of series; laterality missing in about half of studies; a study has 3 to 14 series and the most common series combination covers only 40% of studies (a missing-series mask is needed); slices are ~0.3 mm in-plane vs ~3.5 mm between slices. — [741310](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/rsna-knee-abnormality-detection/discussion/741310#3524307)
- bf16 pitfall, 2026-09-29, Md Kishor Morol: `torch.autocast(dtype=bfloat16)` on Kaggle T4/P100 is emulated (~50x slower per pass), the time guard returned constant 0.5 for more than half the hidden test, LB 0.710 vs 0.851 after switching to fp16; a commenter notes `torch.cuda.is_bf16_supported(including_emulation=False)` is needed because the default may still return True. — [744230](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/rsna-knee-abnormality-detection/discussion/744230#3529827)
- Other negative results: DINOv3 worse than DINOv2, and a report-derived auxiliary loss hurt "no matter how much I played with its weight" (Rasoul Mojtahedzadeh, 2026-09-08) — [740162](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/rsna-knee-abnormality-detection/discussion/740162#3522386). DINOv2-S to DINOv2-B moved CV only +0.0011 against a 0.0020 noise floor, and RAD-DINO lost because the ImageNet mean/std were hard-coded instead of read from `preprocessor_config.json` (stevenleehans, 2026-08-14) — [735154](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/rsna-knee-abnormality-detection/discussion/735154#3512730). Raptor/DINO/gold comparisons from SpeedSci are in Key Question 3.
- A report-embedding linear probe (BGE-M3, PCA 128) scored 0.8423 on gold vs 0.8843 for LLM+regex extraction. Swapping the extraction LLM is worth nothing: qwen2.5:14b 0.8619, qwen3:14b 0.8572, qwen3:30b-a3b 0.8677 on the 58 gold. Extraction caught 207 of 240 gold positives, and 22 of the 33 misses were reports that never name the finding. Going from 24 to 32 cached slices per series (69% to 84% of slices retained) "was worth more to me than anything I did to the labels". — [743148](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/rsna-knee-abnormality-detection/discussion/743148#3528004) (poster not shown by the messages API)

### Inferences
- Our `knee.py` `series_headers()` sorts by `ImagePositionPatient[ax]` (fixed axis per plane) then EchoNumbers and InstanceNumber, falling back to InstanceNumber, so the filename-order bug does not apply. This is not a candidate explanation for our LB-vs-gold gap (checked in `D:\kaggle\rsna-knee\knee.py` lines 92-123).
- Public-stack training code that sorted by filename would still be self-consistent at test time (same ordering), so the willyp74 effect is relevant mostly to people retraining from those baselines. His +0.0276 is a single-fold figure from a pipeline at a much lower AUC level (0.77-0.80), so it should not be extrapolated to the 0.94+ regime.
- With no host answer on RadImageNet or public-weight eligibility, our RadImageNet R50 member is a prize-eligibility risk only if we reach prize ranks; at ~rank 296 (HANDOFF) it is not currently decision-relevant.

## Key Question 2: Which public notebooks have the highest scores now, and what do they add?

### Takeaway
The public frontier is still 0.943 (the DINOv2 + A5 + RadImageNet + Raptor + 4-way CoAtNet "v5/D4" family and its many forks). The only higher figure is the 0.946 parent notebook, which gets there by blending a PRIVATE ConvNeXt ensemble at 10% (+0.003 over the public pipeline). Public gains from 0.937 to 0.943 came from more CoAtNet readers, denser window sampling and small weight changes, not from new base models. Dread Development's Raptor work gives the clearest published evidence on slice density and window count.

### Inferences
- The 0.943 block is a very large (>=50-checkpoint) graph whose last +0.006 came from slice-window density, extra CoAtNet readers and weight tuning, so a modest own ensemble cannot expect to beat it alone; the realistic use of our models is as a decorrelated blend component.
- The one data point on blending a different-family ensemble into the public stack is +0.003 at a 10% weight (0.943 to 0.946, private ConvNeXt). Our own result at a 0.4 weight was +0.001 (0.943 to 0.944), so the weight may be too high or our members too correlated with the stack's RadImageNet heads.
- Our N_SLICE=31 cache with all centres at inference is already at or near the density Dread found best; our finding that PREP 2 (wider band) gave no gain matches his "density, not span" result.
- Ziad's RadImageNet channel result matches our setup (centre slice x3), so RadImageNet is correctly configured; our "no gain at 288px for RadImageNet" is independent evidence rather than a contradiction.

## Key Question 3: What have high-ranked participants said moved their score?

### Takeaway
The top single-model reports (0.947-0.954 LB) are small, ordinary backbones (ResNet-50/small ResNet at 224, CoAtNet at 224/288, a LoRA-tuned Qwen3.5-2B VLM at 384) whose gain they attribute to labels and OOF pseudo-labels (typically blended 50/50), to regularisation/augmentation, and to training on all data. Reports about label-source effects conflict; the one consistent warning is that local CV, gold-58 and LB can disagree.

## Key Question 4: Efficiency-prize facts and fastest known runtimes

### Takeaway
The efficiency score is an AUC term minus a tiny runtime term, so 0.01 AUC is worth about 11.7 minutes at the current maximum AUC. The host's Efficiency LB (data to 2026-10-02 21:40 UTC) lists Scott Willis (0.959), Lukas Nissen Molvær (0.956) and ChenyuWang0628 (0.953) as the top three, but gives no runtimes. Reported fast runtimes: ~5 min for a single ResNet at 0.949, ~23 min for a single ConvNeXt-T fold at 0.941, <10 min for a CoAtNet after DICOM-reading optimisations.

### Cited Findings
- Official formula: Efficiency = AUC / (Benchmark - maxAUC) + RuntimeSeconds / 32400, minimise; Benchmark = sample_submission score, maxAUC = best Private LB AUC; eligible only if the submission is among the team's selected final submissions (or auto-selected) and beats the benchmark on the Private LB; a submission can win both tracks. During the competition the leaderboard notebook shows rank only. — [Efficiency Prize Evaluation](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/rsna-knee-abnormality-detection/overview/efficiency-prize-evaluation)
- Staff (Ryan Holbrook), 2026-08-07: GPU notebooks are eligible; RuntimeSeconds is wall time from notebook execution start to end, including package installs, model loading and DICOM reading. — [733475](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/rsna-knee-abnormality-detection/discussion/733475#3509911)
- Efficiency LB output `full_leaderboard.csv` (file created 2026-10-03 21:49 UTC, newest submission in it 2026-10-02 21:40 UTC; 4,874 rows; columns EfficiencyRank, TeamName, PublicScore, DateSubmitted; no runtime column): rank 1 Scott Willis 0.959; 2 Lukas Nissen Molvær 0.956; 3 ChenyuWang0628 0.953; 4 Brandon Low 0.957; 5 TomKroumov 0.957; 6 Avinash Niroula 0.952; 7 Marco Ledda 0.952; 8 ikevayansky 0.952; 9 Optimo 0.956; 10 Claudio Verdú Ruiz 0.950; 11 Cemal Ataş & Gülşah Ataş 0.956; 13 Pa3฿aJluHа 0.958; 17 rhoskeri 0.957. 86 rows have PublicScore >= 0.950 and 35 have >= 0.955; the top-100 median PublicScore is 0.942 and 57 of the top 100 are below 0.945. - Speed recipe (Prateek Grover, 2026-09-25; CoAtNet, ~40 min to under 10 min): one model copy per GPU with its own study shard instead of DataParallel; read each DICOM once, concurrently (1 reader ~59 MB/s from /kaggle/input, 16 threads ~372 MB/s), one threaded pass per series serving both ordering and pixels; parse DICOM headers directly (0.307 to 0.058 ms/file); read pixels from the PixelData offset with `np.frombuffer` (1.22 to 0.33 ms/slice, bit-identical to pydicom); 1st/99th percentiles on a strided subsample (np.percentile's sort was ~60% of decode time); fp16 weights with half inputs and no autocast (2.08x over autocast on a T4); uint8 to GPU, normalise on device, pinned memory, non-blocking copies, persistent workers. ### Inferences
- With maxAUC ~0.963 the AUC coefficient is 1/(0.5-0.963) = about -2.16 per unit AUC, so 0.01 AUC = 0.0216 = ~700 s (~11.7 min) of runtime, and a 0.015 AUC deficit to Scott Willis (0.959 vs our 0.944) = ~17.5 min. This confirms the HANDOFF conclusion: efficiency engineering cannot compensate for a 0.015 AUC gap; the maxAUC value will change with the final Private LB.
- Rank order in the CSV (rank 3 at 0.953 above ranks 4 and 5 at 0.957) implies runtime differences of at least ~5 min between those teams; top-3 runtimes are therefore likely in the single-digit-minute to low-tens-of-minutes range, but that is inference, not data.
- The efficiency CSV appears to list a team's best-efficiency submission, not its best AUC: "Ayush Kasare" shows 0.916 while our best public score is ~0.944. This matches the HANDOFF entry for EffNet v1 alone (0.916), but I could not confirm the selection rule.
- If we ever target efficiency, the cheap wins above (per-GPU model copies, threaded header+pixel reads, fp16 weights, no pydicom for headers) are all inference-side and independent of model quality; our DICOM read is the same CPU-bound path every team hit.

- Whether the Efficiency LB uses best-AUC or best-efficiency submission per team is unconfirmed.

## Key Question 5: What do these findings imply for our pipeline (derived, not sourced claims)?

### Takeaway
No source describes a missing component that our stack lacks outright; the most defensible experiments are (a) blending a different-family own model into the public stack at a small weight, (b) multi-teacher pseudo-label rounds, (c) heavier pseudo-label weight and train-on-everything single models, and (d) keeping fp16 and physical sorting as they are.

### Cited Findings
(all facts for this section are cited under Key Questions 1-4; the two checks of our own repo are `D:\kaggle\rsna-knee\knee.py` lines 92-123 (physical sort) and `D:\kaggle\rsna-knee\HANDOFF.md`)

### Inferences
1. Slice order: already physical in `knee.py`; no action. Add nothing for the filename trap.
2. bf16: `CLAUDE.md` already mandates fp16; the public stack text contradicts the PSA on this; keep fp16.
3. Blend weight: try our own different-family ensemble (e.g. ConvNeXt-T 224 or CoAtNet ImageNet-init, which several people report at 0.930-0.937 solo) at ~0.10-0.15 of the public stack rather than 0.4; the only published analogue (+0.003 at 10%) used ConvNeXt. This costs one submission per weight and the HANDOFF already suggests <=2 submissions for calibration.
4. Pseudo-labels: three top-50 teams credit OOF teacher blends (Raymond Yuen multi-source teachers, Archit Konde heavier model weight, tennogh OOF pseudo-labels). Our existing `pseudo=a:k1,k2` supports multiple teachers; a round using eff320 OOF + rad + eff31-mil as teachers at 0.5-0.7 is directly supported by their reports (and by the HANDOFF), but one reporter saw no LB lift.
5. Train-on-everything: SpeedSci and Dread Development report a single full-data model about equal to 5-fold, and Raymond Yuen trains on all data with gold only for selection; this would save GPU hours versus 5 folds (we cannot get OOF from it, so it only works after a design is fixed by fold experiments).
6. No reported gain from larger encoders (DINOv2-B +0.0011 within noise; Qwen-VLM single fold 0.950 is the one outlier and needs ~3.5 h/fold on rented GPUs).
7. Deadlines to plan around: merge by 2026-10-15, final selection by 2026-10-22; our weekly GPU quota resets 2026-10-10 (HANDOFF).

### Gaps
- No evidence on whether any of these steps would move us from ~0.944 toward the 0.955-0.963 band; no source describes the recipe of the 0.955+ teams.
