# 2nd Place Solution

Topic 752370, author azamat1ch (https://www.kaggle.com/azamat1ch), posted 2026-10-24T15:50:03.394Z, votes 21

**2nd place. Private 0.960, public 0.969.**

Our sincere thanks to RSNA for building this dataset, to the radiologists who graded every test exam from the images, and to Kaggle for hosting. This was our first serious competition, and a stressful one: submissions that crashed on the hidden test, a long stretch of 0.001 steps, and a **final (winning) submission** sent in the last slot of the last day.

## 1. Solution overview

* **Labels.** An LLM read each radiology report, a second LLM (Astra) read every training exam's images with the report, and our own models' predictions were mixed in last, all as soft labels. Scored against 58 expert-labelled exams, the labels went from 0.899 (reports only) to 0.956.
* **Models.** Six 2.5D MIL models, each cutting its own close-ups of the knee and pooling them into one score per finding.
* **Ensemble.** A rank blend with weights set by hand. Four core members vote on all 12 findings; two specialists add a 0.20 share on PF OA and on Lateral Meniscus and Lateral OA.

## 2. The problem

Training has reports only; the test is graded by experts reading the images. On the 58 training exams with expert labels ("the 58"), the experts mark 240 positives where the reports state 154 (PF OA: 21 against 2), mostly mild disease a report has no reason to mention. The real task was telling mild from normal, and the reports could not teach it.

## 3. Labels

Only training exams were labelled; nothing at inference uses an LLM (the host allowed commercial LLM APIs, thread 733965). We scored every label version on the 58 like a model. A score there moves by about ±0.02 by chance, so we tuned on 24 and kept 34 to check each frozen version; later rules, such as the PF OA floor, used all 58, so those gains are clues, not proof.

### 3.1 Reading the reports

GPT-6.1 Sol read each report in its own language. Each language is roughly one hospital, so a translation slip would teach the model the hospital, not the knee. For each finding it quotes the report, picks one of six answers (yes, probably yes, unclear, probably no, no, not mentioned) and gives a 0-100 score. Code turns the answer and score into a soft label. These labels scored 0.899 on the 58.

### 3.2 Reading the images

An early pilot said images would not help, but it had shown the model only 44 of 134 slices. So Astra (GPT-6 Astra) got every slice: one call per training exam, about 277 images, with the report and the reader's answers. It was told to treat the report as a colleague's read with known gaps.

![](https://www.googleapis.com/download/storage/v1/b/kaggle-user-content/o/inbox%2F26332527%2F1e7a59e1b1913447263d3ff40b5d206d%2Ffig02-astra-sees.png?generation=1792856356496212&alt=media)
*Every series first, then larger close-ups; the number above a close-up stack is the most slices it holds.*

Astra's labels scored 0.942 against the reader's 0.899 (+0.043, 95% interval +0.025 to +0.063). This was our largest improvement in label agreement on the 58 expert-labelled exams. Effusion rose from 0.806 to 0.975, Baker's cyst from 0.803 to 0.979.

### 3.3 Two fixes and a second opinion

On PF OA the reader beat Astra, 0.960 against 0.930: reports describe damaged kneecap cartilage that the experts count as PF OA. So the PF OA label takes half its value from the reader. On Lateral OA a blind pass over the outer half of the knee, without the report, lifted Lateral OA from 0.856 to 0.898.

Then five teacher models trained on these labels, and their out-of-fold predictions were mixed back in. The original label keeps 0.7 where the report is the better witness (ACL, MCL, both menisci, PF OA, Lateral OA) and 0.4 elsewhere. Last came one rule from the 58: all 12 exams there that the reader called "unclear" on PF OA were positive for the experts, so in training every such exam gets at least 0.8.

### 3.4 Who trained on what

The final models train on all 4,403 usable exams, the 58 included with their expert labels. lateral-effnet stays off the 58, so it can still be checked there.

| Member | Labels |
| --- | --- |

| lateral-effnet | Astra's labels with both fixes |
| pf-specialist | lateral-effnet's labels with a gentler PF OA floor |
| window-effnet | pf-specialist's labels, mixed 0.3 with mil-reader's out-of-fold predictions |

## 4. Inputs

Every member turns an exam into 2.5D pictures through the same steps, then cuts its own close-ups.

![](https://www.googleapis.com/download/storage/v1/b/kaggle-user-content/o/inbox%2F26332527%2Fb328183865d408a37de0ff62e6a1408d%2Ffig05-knee-centre.png?generation=1792856420507275&alt=media)
*The input steps every exam goes through, in training and at test.*

mil-reader and pf-specialist came with the team merge and do the first three steps their own way. They split series by fat suppression, read the knee's side from the file tags (a small classifier trained on fastMRI decides unclear knees) and take the knee centre from the headers alone. Their close-ups follow heliosli's public recipe.

![](https://www.googleapis.com/download/storage/v1/b/kaggle-user-content/o/inbox%2F26332527%2F8cf80d615be3ac4ae93c6c1b50a197ad%2Ffig06-member-views.webp?generation=1792856432568447&alt=media)
*The scan shows where each member cuts, slice by slice; each row shows the pictures it reads.*

The members differ most here, and the blend gets its value from that. One input bug cost more than most model changes: our first models read only 4 pictures per series, and reading 15 gave +0.033 on the 58. Since then every member spreads its pictures over the whole box range (mil-reader: 8 sampled slice groups per view during training, 12 evenly spaced groups at test), and at test our four average three slice offsets (0, 1, 2).

## 5. Models

All six encode each 2.5D picture and pool the exam per finding with attention; mil-reader and pf-specialist put a two-layer transformer over the pictures first.

### 5.1 The core four

* **mil-reader**, our best single member (five seeds). It grew from goodpjw2008's public 2.5D ConvNeXt-T MIL reader (Apache 2.0), which scored 0.955 public on our labels. We added the left-knee mirror, attention per finding inside every picture, and knee-centred close-ups. Its encoder was first pretrained on fastMRI knee scans with fastMRI+ boxes.
* **crop-convnext**, built from scratch by us and our simplest strong model: three joint close-ups (side, front, top-down) cut around our knee-centre rule at 384 px, three seeds.
* **window-convnext**: the window views of heliosli's public notebook (a full series view plus local views around the knee), trained our way: our labels and knee centre, every window kept, all data, three seeds. It is our best fold model on the 58 (0.9488).
* **window-effnet**, the same views on EfficientNetV2-S with frozen batch norm. Its labels lean partly on mil-reader, but it sees the knee through other views, so it disagrees with mil-reader instead of copying it.

### 5.2 Two specialists

PF OA and Lateral OA were the weakest findings for every model, at about 0.92 public. So two members vote only on their own findings. To score one finding alone, we sent a probe: that finding real, the other 11 set to 0.5.

* **lateral-effnet** lifted Lateral OA to 0.932 on a public probe, against 0.920 for mil-reader. On private it fell behind (about 0.944 against 0.956), but it is the member least like the others (rank correlation about 0.92).
* **pf-specialist** is mil-reader's encoder with a second head that predicts kneecap cartilage grades, learned from MOAKS grades in the Osteoarthritis Initiative (OAI). Its PF OA probe read 0.944 public and about 0.932 private, against 0.920 and 0.908 for window-convnext. Its weight was fixed before the probe.

## 6. Ensemble

Each member's predictions become ranks per finding across the test set. The ranks are summed with the hand-set weights from the overview table, and we never searched them. On their findings the specialists take 0.20 and the core weights shrink by 0.8. The core four's submission is written first; each specialist runs within a time budget and is dropped if it fails. An unreadable exam gets a default (our members: training-set rates).

## 7. Results

![](https://www.googleapis.com/download/storage/v1/b/kaggle-user-content/o/inbox%2F26332527%2F22d307d3115e17408f083017e875bbdf%2Ffig08-score-progression.png?generation=1792856518354738&alt=media)
*Filled dots are public, rings are private; the dashed rule marks the team merge.*

Better labels and inputs took our own models to 0.964 public; after that only new members helped. Public and private moved together: for submissions at 0.955 public or more, the two boards agreed on the order (rank correlation 0.977). Our chosen submission was also our best private of all 101.

## 8. What didn't work

* **Showing Astra the experts' answers.** It copied them, and a rule it wrote from them scored 0.80 when used blind.
* **More blind one-finding passes.** +0.066 on the 24 tuning exams, then −0.019 on the 34 held back.
* **Tuned blend weights.** They lost 0.002 to 0.007 to fixed weights on held-out exams.
* **A close-up alone.** −0.010 on Lateral Meniscus until the whole knee was added back.
* **Look-alike members.** A model at 0.959 public alone added nothing to the blend.
