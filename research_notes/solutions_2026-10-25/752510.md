## Overview

Only **58 of 4,407 training studies** had expert-adjudicated labels. The remaining studies had multilingual reports, but the evaluation target was an image-based diagnosis. We built **multimodal soft labels**, trained **2.5D MRI readers**, and combined complementary configurations with **group-aware probability blending**. Reports and annotation models were used only to construct supervision; deployed readers used MRI alone.

## 1. Building supervision from imperfect reports

### 1.1 Read the images, not just the diagnostic terms

Reports can omit findings or describe changes below the competition's positive threshold. We asked **GPT-6 Astra to review MRI and the report** and assign 12 continuous targets. Finding-specific readers then refined **PF OA, Lateral OA, and Synovitis**.

For the PF specialist, ordered sheets held up to nine sampled 384-px slices from one plane/sequence group. The reader examined images first, checked location, severity and extent, then reconciled the report. Other report-reader opinions were supporting evidence, not votes. Accepted revisions changed only the relevant targets. Students trained on frozen snapshots, without reports or reader explanations.

### 1.2 Preserve complementary targets

**N** is the PF OA-refined target set. **R** additionally uses specialist rankings for accepted Lateral OA / Synovitis revisions. Within each revised subset, ranks select quantiles of the previous targets, preserving the new ordering while limiting scale changes. Unrevised entries remain fixed. A new reader can change both ordering and probability scale; quantile mapping separates those effects. This is applied only inside each accepted revision subset, not to predictions across the test set. Both versions enter the model pool; **R denotes a target transformation, not a ranking loss**.

### 1.3 External OAI supervision

Selected readers also trained on MRI from the **[Osteoarthritis Initiative (OAI)](https://www.niams.nih.gov/grants-funding/funded-research/osteoarthritis-initiative)**, from a pool of approximately **9,440 knees**. Its expert readings do not completely match the competition targets. The main *v3kd50* recipe mixes applicable expert targets and ten-model teacher targets 50:50 on reliably mapped cells; other cells retain their fill values or reduced weights.

Before mixing, the teacher probability is deweighted as `t=p/[p+w_c(1-p)]`, where `w_c` is its positive-class BCE weight. Some runs also apply an OAI-only prevalence correction to training logits, absent at test time. G36-Gold includes **13 RSNA-only readers** and **23 OAI-mixed readers**, with different per-epoch OAI budgets (Section 7).

## 2. Reading MRI across planes and slices

### 2.1 Geometry and local context

Slices are sorted by **DICOM physical geometry**, knee orientation is standardized, and missing views are masked. The five acquisition groups are sagittal fluid-sensitive, sagittal non-fluid-sensitive, coronal fluid-sensitive, coronal non-fluid-sensitive, and axial. A roughly **140 mm field of view** is resized to 224, 384 or 512 px. Legacy and native single-resampling pipelines remain paired with their original checkpoints. A representative cache allocates 18, 14, 12, 8 and 12 center positions to these groups respectively, up to 64 in total, with missing positions masked. Each checkpoint retains its own preprocessing and sampling configuration.

A **2.5D window stacks three immediately adjacent source slices from one series**, as shown below. Each window is encoded by BiT ResNetV2-50, ConvNeXt-Small, DINOv3 ViT-B/16 or CoAtNet. Class-specific spatial pooling, global average pooling and attention MIL aggregate the window evidence into 12 study logits.

### 2.2 Depth attention and OA coupling

**Depth context (31/36 members)** uses 32-dimensional projections, 2×2 regional pooling and four attention heads within each series. Its training bags preserve series blocks, capped at 32 windows. Inference covers all valid cached windows, not every raw DICOM slice.

**OA coupling (5/36 members)** learns regional cartilage grades, not lesion boxes. Figure 3c shows how cumulative ordinal predictions become compartment-wise maxima and learned residuals for the three OA logits. Other nine logits stay unchanged. OAI grades supervise training only; the coupled prediction head remains active at test time.

## 3. Training and configuration diversity

We use **positive-weighted soft-target BCE** with *mass-preserving* normalization: inverse target-dependent positive-weight mass, scaled by its class-wise training mean. Targets stay continuous. Members use a **fixed 25-epoch endpoint**, not the best Gold58 epoch. For example, the RK4C recipe configures OneCycle with a nominal 50-epoch horizon and applies an epoch-18-to-25 tail ending at 2% of peak learning rate; training stops at epoch 25. Individual members retain their recipe-specific schedule settings. Final members are full-data recipe/seed variants, not one uniform five-fold ensemble.

| Component | Typical setting |
| --- | --- |
| Optimization | AdamW; backbone LR 3e-5 to 1e-4; head LR 1e-3 |
| Schedule | OneCycle-based; epoch-18-to-25 annealing tail |
| Effective batch / weight decay | Typically 8 / 0.01 |
| Augmentation | Intensity, affine, scale and gamma; recipe-specific interpolation |
| Checkpoints | EMA 0.999 or fixed-endpoint Raw |
| Inference | FP16; chunked window encoding |

G36-Gold contains **17 BiT, 7 ConvNeXt, 7 DINOv3 and 5 CoAtNet checkpoints**. Resolution counts are **4 / 23 / 9** at 224 / 384 / 512 px; readouts are **32 EMA and 4 Raw**. These counts do not determine the final weights.

### Diversity with an explicit budget

**Matrix20** allocates five configurations to each backbone family, covering resolution, N/R targets, OAI dose and input processing. It is a curated set, not a full factorial search. Exact entries are in the appendix.

This structure prevents the number of experiments or seeds for one architecture from automatically determining its share. It does **not** make the full ensemble backbone-balanced: Mature12 emphasizes mature BiT recipes, and Expert8 consists entirely of BiT variants. Configuration differences provide candidate diversity, not proof of independent errors.

## 4. G36-Gold: fixed group budgets, finding-specific support

Three groups serve different roles: **Mature12** anchors the prediction with strong established recipes; **Matrix20** supplies balanced backbone coverage; **Expert8** adds RK4/RK4C-family support for **Lateral OA, PF OA and Synovitis**.

The experts generally remain twelve-output models; “expert” describes which predictions receive extra ensemble weight, not necessarily a single-class training objective. Likewise, Matrix20 balances architecture shares within its branch rather than optimizing a separate weight for every checkpoint.

For sigmoid probabilities `p[m, c]`, the base prediction is:

```
B[c] = 0.5 * mean(p[m, c] for m in Mature12)
     + 0.5 * mean(p[m, c] for m in Matrix20)
```

The other nine findings use `B[c]`. The weak three use:

```
P[c] = 0.5 * B[c] + 0.5 * mean(p[m, c] for m in Expert8)
```

Two RK4 seeds and two RK4C seeds belong to both Mature12 and Expert8, giving **12 + 20 + 8 − 4 = 36 unique models**. The eight expert members collectively receive **58.33%** of expanded weak-finding weight, including their Mature12 contributions.

### Final Gold58 refits

After freezing G36, we replaced eligible members with refits including the **58 expert-labeled studies**, matching recipe, seed, input processing and readout. Unmatched members retained their original weights: **27 refits + 9 original checkpoints**, with group membership and fusion fixed.

Gold58 had already informed prompt and model development. We did not use post-refit, in-sample Gold58 scores to reselect members or weights; those predictions served execution/parity checks. Section 7 separates development scores from retrospective leaderboard results.

## 5. Alternative submission: hard-routed A21GT

A21GT keeps **21 A21 members** for an equal probability mean on nine findings. Each weak finding instead receives an independent three-expert mean, with no base-blend contribution:

| Finding | Routed specialists |
| --- | --- |
| Lateral OA | U5 tissue-aware RK4, RK4C, ConvNeXt-512 R |
| PF OA | ConvNeXt-512 R + OAI, RK4C-lib, ConvNeXt-512 R + full OAI |
| Synovitis | RK4Call, RK4C (seed 2027), RK4 (seed 2027) |

For the recipe names used here, **RK4** is the BiT-50 depth reader with R targets and 4,000 OAI knees per epoch; **RK4C** adds the OA-coupled head. **RK4C-lib** uses an alternative OAI PF-OA target/weight mapping, **RK4Call** uses the full OAI pool per epoch, and **U5** adds a training-only auxiliary task with 17 tissue fields to RK4.

Shared members reduce the total to **26 checkpoints: 14 refits and 12 originals**. Its **0.968 public / 0.958 private** was below G36-Gold privately. Both composition and fusion differ, so this is not an isolated comparison of soft mixing and hard routing.

## 6. Inference and deployment

Two T4 workers share DICOM decoding and reusable preprocessing, preserving checkpoint-specific inputs. FP16 and chunked encoding control memory. We verified member identities, local/Kaggle visible-test predictions and class-wise fusion. Both offline packages completed scoring within the limit; runtime forecasts are not hidden-test measurements.

## 7. Experiments and results

**Private scores are retrospective.** Gold58 results below use non-Gold-trained models on a repeatedly used development set, not a blind test. Leaderboard precision is three decimals.

### 7.1 OAI targets and training recipes

All runs use BiT-50 depth, seed 2026 and fixed epoch-25 EMA. N/R identify RSNA targets; expert/teacher ratios apply only to mapped OAI cells.

| Run | RSNA targets | OAI / epoch | Expert : teacher | Gold58 | Public | Private |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| E1 baseline | N | None | — | 0.9400 | 0.962 | 0.951 |
| J-v3 | N | 2,000 | 70 : 30 | 0.9446 | 0.963 | 0.953 |
| J-v3-all | N | 9,440 | 70 : 30 | 0.9408 | 0.962 | 0.951 |
| J-kd50-all | N | 9,440 | 50 : 50 | 0.9453 | 0.963 | 0.952 |
| RK4 | R | 4,000 | 50 : 50 | 0.9505 | 0.963 | 0.953 |
| N3RK4-o18 | R | 4,000 through epoch 18 | 50 : 50 | 0.9523 | 0.963 | 0.953 |

**Target mixing is the cleanest pair.** J-v3-all and J-kd50-all differ in OAI target/weight paths: 70:30 versus 50:50, with label-dependent offsets recomputed. Local AUC rose from 0.9408 to 0.9453; both displayed leaderboard scores rose 0.001.

**More OAI was not uniformly better.** At 70:30, the full pool underperformed the 2,000 draw, although more data also meant more optimizer updates. RK4 changes targets and OAI budget. N3RK4-o18 adds auxiliary supervision and an OAI-free tail; its higher local AUC did not improve displayed leaderboard scores. These are not single-factor ablations of every component.
