# External data before final selection: what will be checked, and for whom?
750236 | Michał Choiński (@mikecho) | 2026-10-17T11:59 | votes -4

@javaduke95  @cdcarr  @michelleriopel  @shosys  @ryanholbrook  @macruzbar 

With five days left before the October 22 deadline, I'd like to ask for a short statement about how external data will be checked. It decides how every team uses its two final selections.

What the rules and rulings say

- Rules section 6.a: External Data must be "publicly available and equally accessible to use by all Participants of the Competition for purposes of the competition at no cost to the other Participants", or meet the Reasonableness criteria in 6.b, which names "geo restrictions" as a possible obstacle.

- KneeCoT is explicitly not allowed (734109 ).

- OAI is allowed only "if it is generally accessible to all participants without an escalated (i.e. institutional) review and sign-off" (741819 ).

- "Datasets that require an institutional research agreement or limit access to users with institutional affiliations clearly do not" meet the criteria (743416 ).

- Publicly shared weights are eligible, with their source datasets disclosed (744056 ). That answer concerned weights trained on competition data.

Where this leaves the commonly used datasets

Measured against those criteria, several of them look doubtful:

- OAI: NIH blocks access from several countries, including China, which is eligible here (748276 , 749145 ).

- fastMRI: an application stating affiliation and intended use.

- MRNet and KneeMRI: licences that forbid derivative works.

- KneeXNet: derived from OAI.

None of these has received a yes or no.

What is happening in practice

- External data clearly moves the score. One public write-up reports +0.005 public AUC from adding OAI knees. Near the medal lines that is hundreds of places.

- Many of the most-forked public notebooks combine checkpoints whose dataset cards declare OAI, fastMRI or undisclosed private pretraining. Others carry no training-data statement at all. Teams that fork them often cannot tell what their own submission contains.

- The rules describe compliance checks for prize winners (2.8, 3.9.b), but nothing for medal positions.

So the incentive is simple. If only the top few are checked, a team aiming for a medal gains by using doubtful data and has almost nothing to lose. Teams that read the rules strictly are pushed down the leaderboard by teams that don't. That works against the hosts' own stated wish that "winning models be distinguished by the ingenuity and rigor of their approach rather than by privileged access to resources".

Final selection makes this worse

Without a ruling, the rational choice is to hedge: one final submission with external data and one without. That uses both slots, so there is none left for a smaller, faster model. That is exactly the kind of submission the efficiency track is meant to reward.

Questions (a short yes or no for each would be enough)

- Will external-data compliance be checked only for prize-eligible teams, or also for medal positions on the private leaderboard?

- If a non-compliant submission is found, will it be removed from the private leaderboard, and can medals be withdrawn?

- Are public checkpoints trained partly on external data (OAI, fastMRI, private pretraining) allowed for teams that never accessed that data themselves?

- Does a team that forks a public notebook carry responsibility for undisclosed training data inside its checkpoints?

- Is OAI allowed, given the NIH country restrictions?

- Can the answer be pinned before October 22, so that everyone selects final submissions under the same rule?

A clear answer either way, even "only prize winners will be checked", would let every team decide on the same basis. Thank you for organizing the competition.
