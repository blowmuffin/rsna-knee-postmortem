# The most-forked notebook here is inference-only — and its per-label weights were tuned on a board that can't measure   them
749570 | Shenzai25 | 2026-10-15T11:57 | votes -2

TL;DR — The top public notebook in this competition contains no training code at all: it rank-averages 20 pre-trained members and writes a submission in ~3m37s. Everything that looks like model tuning is actually fusion weight tuning. One of those weights, for Lateral Meniscus, is set to 1.00 — which multiplies the DINOv2, A5 and RadImageNet contributions for that label by zero, leaving one of twelve findings decided by a single stage. I measured how much resolution the public board has, and it cannot validate that choice. Ablation numbers below (run in progress).

No training anywhere. Every weight is loaded from a public dataset or model. The pipeline is: DINOv2 views → A5 attention folds → RadImageNet + feature calibrator → CoAtNet/raptor views → rank-average → submission.csv, in ~3m37s on T4×2.

This is worth stating plainly because it changes what "improving on it" means. If you forked it and you're tuning something, you are tuning fusion weights, not a model. And if you believe the answer is a better model, you have to bring one that isn't in the bundle already.

2. The public board cannot measure what we're tuning

(a) The split is small. The board header states it is calculated on ~30% of the test data, with the final result on the other 70%. Scored to 3 decimals.

Ten consecutive ranks, identical to three decimals. Between 0.941 and 0.956 there are about 2,030 teams — that is roughly 135 teams per thousandth. Ties are resolved by submission time, not by score.

Put those together: a per-label weight change that shifts the public score by less than ~0.002 is inside the noise band of a 30% split, on a board where 270 teams share the same 0.001. Nobody, including the people who copy this notebook, can validate a per-label choice on this board. It is not that the choice is wrong — it is that this instrument has no reading for it.

3. The knob in question

Near the end of the last cell:

_coatnet_weight = {label: 0.60 for label in _blend_labels}
_coatnet_weight.update({
    'ACL': 0.75,
    'Medial Meniscus': 0.80,
    'Lateral Meniscus': 1.00,   # <-- full weight
    'Lateral OA': 0.75,
    'Fracture': 0.75,
})
...
_blend_output[l] = (1.0 - w) * transformer[l] + w * coatnet[l]

With w = 1.00, the blended prediction for Lateral Meniscus is pure CoAtNet. Three of the four stages are multiplied out of that one label. The author's own note in the later re-write of this notebook calls this "the likeliest place to give back points privately" — which is the same conclusion I'm reaching from the resolution argument above.

For the other 11 labels the weight stays at 0.75–0.80, so only this column is fully handed over.

5. What I'd do with the time we have left

If you have forked this notebook unmodified, you are running per-label weights that were selected against a board with no resolution to select them — and the whole configuration is worth ≈0.941, which is the middle of a very flat distribution, not the top of it.

In rough order of expected value:

- Decide deliberately on that one column. Either take the uniform 0.60 (no stage is zeroed) or keep 1.00 and say why. Don't inherit the choice by default.

- Spend remaining submissions on the private side, not the public side. With ~135 teams per thousandth, chasing public score means chasing noise.

- If you add a member, add information, not another copy. The bundle already contains the DINOv2/A5/RadImageNet/CoAtNet/raptor members — re-ranking them is a re-weighting, not a new signal.
