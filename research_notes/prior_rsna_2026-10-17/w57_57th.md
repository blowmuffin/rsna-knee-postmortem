# RSNA Intracranial Aneurysm Detection — My Approach (Top 5%/ 57th)
685172 | Wosheng Deng | 2026-03-27T05:28 | votes 0

The task involves detecting and localizing intracranial aneurysms from multimodal 3D medical imaging data (CTA / MRA / MRI), which is challenging due to:

- Small lesion size  

- Severe class imbalance  

- High variability across imaging modalities  

My approach focuses on improving signal-to-noise ratio through a localization-first 3D pipeline combined with multimodal modeling and ensembling.

Key Idea

Localization-first 3D pipeline to reduce background noise and improve detection of small lesions.

Instead of directly performing classification on full-head scans, I first localize relevant anatomical regions and then apply downstream models on refined Regions of Interest (ROI).

Approach Overview

The solution is built as a three-stage pipeline:

Stage 1 — Brain ROI Localization

- A 3D segmentation model (MedNeXt-S) is applied to extract the brain region from full-head scans.  

- This removes irrelevant background and improves downstream efficiency.  

Stage 2 — Artery ROI Refinement

- Within the brain region, a second-stage model focuses on arterial structures.  

- Anatomical priors (e.g., TotalSeg) are used to guide localization.  

Stage 3 — Aneurysm Classification

Two complementary model families are used:

- Transformer-based 3D models  

- Operate on ROI inputs  

- Capture global spatial features  

- CNN + RNN + Attention models  

- Operate on volumetric sequences  

- Capture temporal/contextual patterns  

Ensemble Strategy

Predictions from multiple models are combined using weighted ensembling, which improves robustness and stabilizes performance across validation folds.

Result

- Final Score: 0.70 Mean Weighted AUC-ROC

The solution shows strong performance on both:

- Primary label (Aneurysm Present)  

- Auxiliary anatomical labels
