# The previous RSNA competition: what happened and what transfers, 2026-10-17

Sources: competition pages, 15 write-ups, 20+ forum threads and both final leaderboards of RSNA Intracranial Aneurysm Detection; the same checks (shorter) on RSNA 2024 Lumbar Spine. Raw notes and numbers: `research_notes/prior_rsna_2026-10-17/` (start at `00_index.md`; leaderboard join in `lb_public_vs_private.csv`, all derived numbers in `shakeup_numbers.md` and `lumbar_2024_shakeup.md`). Read-only: nothing was posted, joined or submitted, no competition data downloaded.

## 1. Identification and facts

**The competition is RSNA Intracranial Aneurysm Detection** (`rsna-intracranial-aneurysm-detection`, id 99552). Evidence: a search of every RSNA-hosted competition by deadline puts it directly before the knee one (2025-07-28 to 2025-10-14; the one before it is RSNA 2024 Lumbar Spine, deadline 2024-10-08, 1,874 teams, same staff and format).

| Item | Aneurysm 2025 | Knee (now) |
|---|---|---|
| Task | 13 aneurysm locations + "Aneurysm Present" from CTA / MRA / MRI T1post / T2 series (about 2,500 test series) | 12 knee findings from multi-sequence MRI |
| Metric | Mean of AUC(Present) and mean AUC of the 13 locations (weights 13:1, so half and half) | Macro AUC over 12 findings |
| Teams / submissions | 1,147 teams, 1,454 participants, 16,298 submissions | 6,143 teams |
| Prizes | $50k: 12 / 10 / 8 / 5 / 3x5 (top 9 paid) | $77k |
| Format | Notebook only, CPU or GPU up to 12 h, no internet; test served one series at a time through an evaluation API, public and private series interleaved in one run | Notebook only, 9 h |
| Timeline | Entry and merge deadline 10-07; final submission 10-14; winners' package (code, video, method) due 10-27; the 8th-place write-up is dated 10-27 | Deadline 10-22 |
| Runs | Every submission ran on the full test set, so the private score exists the moment a run completes; "private LB revealed, not computed" at the end (staff, 607923) | |
| Rules on external data | "Freely and publicly available external data is allowed, including pre-trained models"; rules 2.6: must be publicly available and equally accessible at no cost, or pass the host's "Reasonableness" test (cost, geo-restriction) | |
| Winners | CC-BY-NC 4.0 licence; open code, video, and a forum post with code and weights; prizes "subject to review and verification"; the sponsor may disqualify or require remediation within one week | |

Platform problems shaped the whole competition: the data and hidden set were updated several times (late August, 09-10, 09-30 notices); the host rescored some batches and declined to rescore a change touching under 5% of cases; on 10-01 it said no further data fixes would come; test DICOMs had stripped tags and broken multi-frame files; the last week had heavy queueing and unstable run times (section 6).

Public top 10 finished 2, 5, 1, 11, 4, 3, 7, 10, 6, 14. The winner was public 3rd, the public leader finished 2nd, the public 2nd finished 5th, the public 4th finished 11th.

**Score loss grows with the public score** (private minus public): -0.030 for the six teams at 0.85 or more; -0.019 at 0.80-0.85; -0.014 at 0.75-0.80; -0.008 at 0.71-0.75; about -0.0002 for the copy block. The 2nd-place team traced a steady 0.03-0.04 public-to-private gap to the share of irregular multi-frame DICOMs, which it believes is lower in the private subset (the gap appeared only after it handled those files properly instead of returning 0.5). The 11th-place team saw CV 0.89-0.90, public 0.87, private 0.81-0.82 and could not explain it; a commenter guessed a domain shift that broke its brain segmenter. In lumbar the private log loss was worse by +0.06 for the public top 30 while the order held.

### The "public notebook crowd" (the analogue of our 0.958 block)
- The dominant public notebook was the "32ch img infer [LB 0.69]" one (730 votes). **350 teams sat at public 0.6905-0.6915 and 246 at exactly 0.69126.** Of those 246, 229 finished at an identical private 0.69103. The private tie block at 0.69103 held 263 teams over **private ranks 245-507** (21st to 44th percentile): no medal anywhere near it (bronze ended at 114).
- Within an identical-score block the order follows submission time only loosely (rank correlation about 0.5 with the team's last-submission date: 0.50 over the 263 aneurysm teams, 0.54 over a 278-team lumbar block; the date used is the team's last submission, so this is a proxy): a **late copy tends to land in the back half** of its block. The rules say ties go to the earlier submission.
- Lumbar had the same structure: 437 teams at public 0.481 (public ranks 193-629); private ranks median 417, 5th-95th percentile 212-633, 11 teams (2.5%) reached the bronze zone, none the silver zone.
- **Copies that tweaked something won or lost sizeably**: 14 of the 246 had changed behaviour on the private part only (same public score, private from 0.597 to 0.700); 3 reached the bronze zone (private ranks 78, 88, 95).
- **Teams that squeezed a small public gain out of that family lost on private.** Public 0.70-0.71 (public ranks about 80-150): median private-minus-public -0.011, 20% ended below the pure copy. The big public-top-100 collapses (100 to 631, 79 to 594, 92 to 603, 95 to 515, 110 to 658, 156 to 734) are almost all this group; two of the names are authors of public blend or transfer-learning notebooks of that family.
- **A modest, honest build on the public ecosystem could medal.** The 55th-place team (silver) retrained the public notebook's pipeline with the shared training code and a new backbone, added flip TTA with a left-right label remap, and sat at LB about 0.71. The 38th (silver) built its own 3D U-Net heatmap model and stayed at 0.73 on both boards ("consistent CV, public, private" was its own finding).
- 124 teams sat at exactly 0.5 (failed or constant submissions).

### Patterns across the top
- **Localise first, then classify a crop.** Seven of the top 9 cut the input to a brain or vessel region before the main model (segmentation, detection, box regression or a fixed rule); 8th and 9th work slice-wise with detection and hard-negative sampling. Whole-volume models stayed near 0.7 (3rd place: loss unstable and score never above 0.7 on whole volumes; 4th: the crop took the baseline from 0.7 to 0.8).
- **Two families won, with overlapping scores**: 3D nnU-Net with auxiliary tasks (1st, 2nd, 7th), 3D ResNet-18 on a detected crop (3rd), and 2.5D slice models with strong negative sampling (4th, 5th, 6th, 8th, 9th, 11th).
- **Heterogeneous ensembles held up on private even when public did not show it**: 3rd place's teammate found that adding a 3D EfficientNetV2 raised private only (it was left out of the final because public did not move); 9th's parallel meta-model beat the series one on CV (0.852 vs 0.84) and private (0.823 vs 0.81); 6th and 4th gained about 0.01 CV from 2-3 members.
- **Left-right flip with label swap** was common: training augmentation in 2nd, 5th (+0.01), 6th, 8th, 15th; flip TTA in 1st, 2nd (8x) and 55th (+0.005). Two teams deliberately avoided mirroring in training because the labels encode left and right: the 7th and, for its fine segmentation models, the 1st.
- **Label and data cleaning paid**: 1st removed about 60 series; 2nd corrected left-right and supra/infra-clinoid mix-ups; 5th relabelled 33 negatives; lumbar 2nd got +1% from dropping labels the OOF ensemble disagreed with.
- **What did not work**: whole-volume 3D models; vessel segmentation as a classifier input (3rd); modality/plane auxiliary heads (3rd, although a modality head helped 2nd); MIL, LSTM and decoder heads (3rd); end-to-end CNN + RNN (6th); resizing the slice axis (9th); isotropic 1 mm resampling and nnDetection, which was better on public but worse on private (7th); ConvNeXt under half precision, which went to NaN (9th); external ADAM and LargeIA data (ruled out by the host).
- **Five of the top nine report last-days timeouts or runtime instability** (2nd, 6th, 7th, 8th, 9th, and the 11th as well); three submitted a smaller ensemble or older checkpoint than intended: 2nd (two folds), 7th (1500 of 3000 epochs), 9th (two of five folds). 8th had about half of its submissions time out and 6th dropped a 3D branch for runtime instability. The submitted model was often the best one that finished.

## 5. Compliance and external-data enforcement history
- **Host rulings came as explicit forum answers.** On 2025-09-22 the host ruled that the Large Intracranial Aneurysm Dataset (Zenodo, needs institution details) and ADAM (confidentiality terms) did NOT qualify; the host had filed its own access request without an institution and was refused. On 09-24 the Lausanne and Royal Brisbane TOF-MRA sets on OpenNeuro (CC0, direct download) were confirmed as usable. Test used: reachable without institutional status or special permission, and no use restriction.
- **Everything in the top 9 that used outside data used open sets and said so**: 2nd (TopCoW, TopBrain; excluded LargeIA), 5th (the two OpenNeuro sets), 6th (OpenNeuro, open skull data), 11th (TotalSegmentator training sets from Zenodo). The 7th-place team co-trained with ADAM and LargeIA, found out it was not allowed and dropped it before the final.
- **No post-deadline disqualification of a top-12 team is visible**: the team names on the final board match the write-ups for places 1, 2, 4, 5, 6, 7, 8, 9 and 11, and the top-9 write-ups with code were posted by 10-27 (the 8th on that date). The host's recap says it "cleaned the leaderboard and disqualified some teams" but gives no list, no number and no reason; the lumbar recap carries the identical sentence, so it is Kaggle boilerplate. Team count: the page says 1,147 and the cleaned board has 1,148 rows, so the number removed cannot be recovered.
- **Checking at medal level beyond prizes: no evidence.** The rules only promise review for prizes; a disqualified team "may be removed from the leaderboard" and medals "may not be awarded". Winners had 13 days after the deadline to deliver code, video and a forum post with code and weights.
- Public notebook sharing was the norm (private sharing outside a team is banned, public sharing is allowed); no enforcement against copying a public notebook is visible.

## 6. Final selection and runtime lessons
- **Selection closes at the deadline and every pick must be fully scored by 23:59 UTC.** A reply on 611750 says a submission still running does not count, and the host's last statement was that the deadline would not move, citing fairness to teams that finished on time. Teams that submitted "in time" but were still running when the clock hit zero could not select the result.
- **Unselected = automatic choice.** Teams worried about the "autoselect", which on Kaggle defaults to the best public scores. On this board the private winner was public 3rd and the public 1st finished 2nd, so at the very top public order was a decent guide.
- **Runtime was non-deterministic.** The same deterministic code took 8 h one day and timed out at 12 h the next (several reports); staff said queue waiting does not count against the limit and could not tell whether the slowdowns were a platform fault or variance in the submitted code; one team's in-notebook guard at 11 h 55 min did not stop runs that reached 14-18 h; 9th place had a 21-hour (wall clock, including queue) submission finish in the last minute and selected it. Staff advice on budgeting: divide the 12 h over the roughly 2,500 test series, with a failsafe default prediction. 7th place: all later checkpoints timed out although only the weights changed.
- **Last 48 hours were congested**: timeouts, queueing and "Kaggle Error" were frequent, per the megathread (44 comments) and the host's own wrap post; no extension.
- **Checkpoint choice on public data hurts**: 5th place picked the epoch on the public LB with 50 validation series, public 0.89 to private 0.847 (public #2 to private #5); 11th's higher-CV self-distilled model was worse on both boards. The 2nd-place ablation table is the counter-case: each of its five steps raised both boards in order (public 0.844, 0.871, 0.888, 0.898, 0.900; private 0.813, 0.825, 0.857, 0.862, 0.867); even the smallest step (+0.002 public, a second fold) was +0.005 private. Real gains transfer; the question for us is whether our union is a real gain of that kind.
- **Selecting on public alone leaves private gains behind**: a 3rd-place teammate's 3D EfficientNetV2 would have helped private but not public, so it was not selected. The reverse also happened (5th place's epoch picked on public).
- **Fallbacks cut both ways**: 1st place used an OOF-mean fallback to protect against segmentation failures; 2nd place removed its "return 0.5 on exception" wrapper and found the real cause of a 0.03-0.04 gap (multi-frame files); 124 teams scored exactly 0.5.

## 7. Transferable lessons for RSNA Knee in the last 5 days

Context (from `reports/2026-10-17 how to win.md` and HANDOFF): the 0.958 block holds about 840 teams (ranks 139-978 of 6,140) and is mostly exact copies of a few notebooks; gold is rank 22 at 0.963, bronze about rank 614; we are at about rank 660; about 4 GPU-hours and 4 submissions a day remain; the two candidate picks are a copy of a 0.958 notebook and a unique union of two public families (our `union` mode, base unchanged plus a guarded second notebook, 8.6 h guard on a 9 h limit).

1. **A copy is insurance, not an upside pick.** All copies of one notebook get the same private score (229 of 246 in aneurysm; 275 of 437 in lumbar). Inside the block, order is submission time plus, in a block of several families, the private noise of that family. A copy submitted now tends to sit in the back half of the sub-block of its notebook, and an 840-team block spans ranks 139-978, i.e. both sides of the bronze line (about rank 614 by the formula). Expect the copy to land at that notebook's private position minus a late-submission penalty, with no way to rise above it.
2. **A unique union breaks the tie but is only worth it if its edge is real.** In aneurysm, small public-LB gains out of one family cost 0.011 privately on the median (the pure copy lost 0.0002) and one fifth of the tweakers ended below the copy. Our edge from a union is small (the how-to-win note estimates +0.001-0.002) versus a public noise floor of about 0.003 and an aneurysm-type shake-up that moved the median team 37 places. Do not choose between union and copy by comparing public scores that differ by less than about 0.003; use the paired argument (same predictions, so the rank difference inside the block is the signal) and prefer the union whose second family is genuinely different. That is also where the top-9 ensembles earned their private gains (heterogeneous families: 3rd, 6th, 9th), not from re-weighting one family.
3. **Make the two picks fail differently.** The final score is the better of two picks, so the second pick has the most value when it is NOT strongly correlated with the first. A union that already carries the base at 50% is close to a copy of that base; a second pick that is the other family alone (or the base alone if the union is mainly the other family) gives more spread. Do not select two near-identical files.
4. **Completion risk is the biggest hazard: three of the top nine had to cut their final ensemble.** The knee guard of 8.6 h on a 9 h limit leaves little room; an aneurysm team's own guard at 11 h 55 min failed to prevent platform timeouts that reached 14 h. Where the union's measured run (the lavinwins run gives L) leaves under 1.5-2 h of slack, shorten the second family (fewer folds or models, cut TTA) rather than rely on the guard. Run the final candidates through a full scored submission by the morning of 10-21 UTC at the latest; staff say a run in progress at 23:59 does not count and queues spike in the last 48 hours.
5. **Never let a fallback hide that a family is missing.** 1st place's OOF fallback is the model to copy (a prior, not 0.5). Our union already keeps the base file on any error; confirm in the log of the scored run that the second family contributed on all studies (the `union_base.csv` and `union_lav.csv` diagnostics) and that the blend actually applied, because a silent skip returns the base alone, which is the copy you were trying to avoid. 2nd place found a 0.03-0.04 public-private gap that a return-0.5 fallback had been hiding (its data-handling defect only showed once the fallback was removed).
6. **Expect a uniform score drop and a mostly stable top.** Aneurysm gold membership was set inside the public top 15 and top teams lost 0.02-0.03 on average; lumbar's top was equally compressed (private #1-#3 within 0.0025) and still kept 8 of the public top 10 in the private top 10. For knee, the band above the block (0.959-0.969, gold at rank 22 = 0.963) is a few thousandths wide, so the rank change from a +0.001-0.002 union is a handful of places, not a jump. Do not burn the last four GPU hours on a new member whose only support is a public-LB delta under 0.003; on the aneurysm board such deltas did not survive (20-43% of the small gainers ended under the plain copy).
7. **Select manually.** Set both final picks yourself before 23:59 UTC on 10-22; the automatic choice is the best public score. Do it after both runs are scored, not on the last evening: in aneurysm the last day produced timeouts for many teams, and any pick that is "running" at the deadline counts for nothing.
8. **Compliance (inference from the precedent).** In aneurysm every top-9 team's outside data was open or ruled eligible by name, the host settled questions by thread within days, and no top finisher was removed. Scrutiny, where it exists, attaches to the winners' package (code, weights and data list within 13 days of the deadline) for prize positions; nothing in either RSNA competition indicates audits of medal-only positions for external data. For knee that means data-licence doubts about a pick (for example the OAI-under-NDA question in the how-to-win note) weigh on prize-range outcomes far more than on a bronze or silver finish.

9. **Net reading.** Keep one low-variance pick (the copy: its private score is fixed in advance by the notebook; its cost is the tie-break position) and one distinct pick (the union) whose completion margin has been measured on a full scored run; spend no more GPU hours or submissions on variants with deltas under 0.003; once both are scored, set the two picks by hand, no later than 10-21 UTC, with as little overlap between the two as possible.
