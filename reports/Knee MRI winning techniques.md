# Diversity and merging beat foundation models

No technique found in the 2024-2026 literature or the forum record is a +0.015 step, and +0.015 is what separates our 0.944 blend (about rank 296) from the rank-10 line at roughly 0.959. Every effect with a documented size on a comparable setup is worth +0.001 to +0.005 on the public leaderboard, and the big RSNA-lineage wins (segmentation-pretrained backbones, localise-then-crop) depend on host-supplied masks or keypoints that the knee data does not have. The best expected-value moves, ranked by gain per unit of effort, are: (1) merge before the 2026-10-15 deadline with a partner whose arm is not the public stack; (2) add a decorrelated ConvNeXt-T-class member and blend it at a low weight; (3) switch from 5-fold to full-data refits once a recipe is fixed, which buys more diversity per GPU-hour; (4) run two cheap, measurable A/Bs (EfficientNet at 320 px, stronger rotation or a slice-level auxiliary loss); and (5) add zero-GPU insurance (fail-safe output, fp16 only, header robustness). DINOv3, OrthoFoundation, MRI-CORE and the medical vision-language models rank below all of these because the evidence is thin, the encoders are too heavy for 2x T4 in fp16 or already sit inside the public stack, and OrthoFoundation and DINOv3 carry eligibility risk. My estimate is that the evidence-backed moves land the blend at 0.947-0.952 (roughly rank 60-190); top-10 odds are in the single-digit percent range unless a merge brings in a partner already at 0.955 or better. The last section maps all of this onto the Oct 10-16 and Oct 17-22 GPU budgets.

## A 0.015 gap is a recipe problem, not a missing technique

The public board puts ranks 6-12 at 0.959 and rank 1 at 0.963 ([Leaderboard](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/rsna-knee-abnormality-detection/leaderboard)), while the documented effect sizes of generic tricks are an order of magnitude smaller than that gap. In RSNA 2025 aneurysm, soft pseudo-label distillation lifted CV by 0.03 but private score by only 0.01 ([4th place](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/rsna-intracranial-aneurysm-detection/writeups/4th-place-solution)); in RSNA 2024 lumbar, a 30-model average improved on the best single model by 0.0215 log-loss ([3rd place](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/rsna-2024-lumbar-spine-degenerative-classification/writeups/sonyspine-s-tkmn-moyashii-3rd-place-solution)), while in RSNA 2023 abdominal trauma ensembling "did not really help" on private ([2nd place](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/rsna-2023-abdominal-trauma-detection/writeups/on-strike-2nd-place-solution)). The effects that were large all rode on localisation supervision: removing segmentation-pretrained backbone weights cost the 2025 aneurysm winner 0.108 AUC in ablation (0.902 to 0.794) and removing the auxiliary segmentation loss cost 0.026 ([1st place](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/rsna-intracranial-aneurysm-detection/writeups/1st-place-solution)); the 2022 cervical-spine winner trained his stage-1 segmenter on 87 masked studies ([1st place](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/rsna-2022-cervical-spine-fracture-detection/writeups/qishen-ha-1st-place-solution)); and ranks 1-6 in 2024 lumbar all depended on host or crowd-sourced keypoints ([community summary](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/rsna-2024-lumbar-spine-degenerative-classification/discussion/541279)). The one RSNA contest without such labels, the 2021 MGMT task, was dominated by noise: identical EfficientNet-B0 configurations scored CV 0.53-0.62 across seeds ([1st place](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/rsna-miccai-brain-tumor-radiogenomic-classification/writeups/tunisia-ai-i-love-this-competition-1st-place-solut)). The knee task supplies 58 gold studies and nothing else, so the winners' recipe cannot be copied.

What the forum shows is that our architecture class is not the ceiling. Self-reported single models in the same family score far above our own components: a plain 224 px five-fold ResNet-50 at 0.954 ([CoolinLai](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/rsna-knee-abnormality-detection/discussion/735304#3529410)), a single-fold 2.5D CoAtNet at 224 px at 0.950 ([Archit Konde](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/rsna-knee-abnormality-detection/discussion/735304#3525193)), and a rank-10 team that reports only small ResNet/EfficientNet models, going from 0.938 single-fold to 0.947 five-fold to 0.949 single-fold and then 0.959 without explaining the last step ([Scott Willis](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/rsna-knee-abnormality-detection/discussion/735304#3521391)). Our own models reach 0.926 and 0.924 alone, 0.936 as a pair and 0.938 as a three-model ensemble ([EXPERIMENTS.md](D:/kaggle/rsna-knee/EXPERIMENTS.md)). These are unverified claims, but they point at labels, pseudo-label pipelines and training recipe rather than at a missing component. Independent evidence says the same about architecture: a report-labelled multi-finding knee system built on ResNet18 plus a slice transformer reached only 0.74-0.85 external AUC on the hard classes we also struggle with (lateral meniscus 0.77, tibial cartilage 0.74) ([Eur Radiol 2026](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13035746)), and the December 2025 AnyMC3D benchmark found that query-based attention pooling over slice embeddings beat average, max, transformer and LSTM fusion, which is the design our MIL head already uses ([AnyMC3D](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.12887)).

Our ability to measure is the binding constraint, and it should decide what gets run. The 58 gold studies carry roughly +/-0.01-0.02 of noise and have misled leaderboard decisions before ([CLAUDE.md](D:/kaggle/rsna-knee/CLAUDE.md)); public-board differences below about 0.003 are noise; and OOF scored against report labels is biased against any change to the targets, as the rubric-label run showed ([EXPERIMENTS.md](D:/kaggle/rsna-knee/EXPERIMENTS.md)). The theory is unforgiving as well: under class-conditional label omission the corrupted-label AUC is an affine function of the clean AUC, so the ranking is unchanged and only the instance-dependent part (site and reporter style) is recoverable ([Menon et al.](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v37/menon15.html)). Our site-only prior scores 0.64 against report labels but 0.59 against gold, which is the signature of exactly that instance-dependent noise, but no local test can tell us whether a fix for it works. This report therefore ranks ideas whose effect OOF can resolve (model family, resolution, augmentation, architecture) above ideas that change targets, and sends the latter to the speculation list.

## Six evidence-backed levers, ranked by gain per effort

The estimates in the table are my judgement from the cited effect sizes, not measurements on this task, and the gains are not additive.

**Merging is the cheapest route to diversity, and the one top teams visibly took.** Several names near the top of the board look like merged teams (joinT fusion, takoi & charmq, Parag & Ian, YC & NB), and the rules set the merger deadline at 2026-10-15 23:59 UTC, a five-person cap, and a combined cap of five submissions per day of team existence ([Leaderboard](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/rsna-knee-abnormality-detection/leaderboard), [Rules](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/rsna-knee-abnormality-detection/rules)). Chris Deotte's endorsed principle is that models which disagree with each other outperform models that agree ([thread 742926](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/rsna-knee-abnormality-detection/discussion/742926#3528357)), and the only published number for blending a different-family ensemble into the public stack is +0.003 at a 10% weight, from a private ConvNeXt ensemble ([yamadan96 notebook](https://www.kaggle.com/code/yamadan96/rsna-knee-d4-public0946)). Two offers are open: one competitor will share an independent DINOv2 arm with anyone at 0.945 or better ([Md Kishor Morol](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/rsna-knee-abnormality-detection/discussion/744230#3529827)), and another at 0.942 solo wants a merge ([KalyanG17](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/rsna-knee-abnormality-detection/discussion/745091#3531605)). The catch is overlap: the first offer (DINOv2-S, 6 slots x 16 slices, 336 px) closely matches the A5 branch already inside the 0.943 stack, which uses DINOv2, 6 slots x 16 slices and 336 px ([rsna-versia-5](https://www.kaggle.com/code/evgendvorkin/rsna-versia-5)), so it would add little. Prefer a partner with a different backbone family and an independent label pipeline, and confirm that the combined members fit one 9-hour notebook on 2x T4.

**A ConvNeXt-T-class member fills the family gap that our own greedy search exposed.** Our ensemble saturates near 0.898 OOF with EfficientNet and RadImageNet members ([EXPERIMENTS.md](D:/kaggle/rsna-knee/EXPERIMENTS.md)), and the public stack's described components are DINOv2, CoAtNet and RadImageNet heads ([rsna-versia-5](https://www.kaggle.com/code/evgendvorkin/rsna-versia-5)). One participant reports an ImageNet ConvNeXt-T at 0.938 five-fold and 0.941 for a single fold in 23 minutes ([XuKong Ji](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/rsna-knee-abnormality-detection/discussion/740375#3522881)), another reports 0.930 on their own labels ([KalyanG17](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/rsna-knee-abnormality-detection/discussion/745091#3531605)), so the spread is wide but the upper end exceeds our whole three-model ensemble. I would swap this in for the ImageNet ResNet-50 A/B planned in the HANDOFF: the one forum A/B on ResNet-50 initialisation found ImageNet 0.063 below RadImageNet in a different pipeline ([Ziad Ahmed](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/rsna-knee-abnormality-detection/discussion/737696)). Two implementation notes follow from our own code: `knee.py` would give ConvNeXt the 3e-4 default learning rate, and 1e-4 (as for CoAtNet and RadImageNet) is the safer starting point; and a 224 px fold should cost about an hour, estimated from the DINOv2-S run at 65-70 minutes per fold with similar FLOPs, so measure it before committing. Our current blend weight on own models is 0.4 and bought +0.001 ([EXPERIMENTS.md](D:/kaggle/rsna-knee/EXPERIMENTS.md)); calibrate 0.10-0.15 against the pending knee-blend-288 result, expecting at most +0.001-0.002 from the weight alone.

**Full-data refits stretch the budget.** Several reports find a single full-training model about equal to a five-fold ensemble: SpeedSci says so directly ([thread 740375](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/rsna-knee-abnormality-detection/discussion/740375#3523175)), Dread Development's Raptor is one CoAtNet trained on all labelled data with a small gold holdout and no folds ([thread 737696](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/rsna-knee-abnormality-detection/discussion/737696#3517697)), and XuKong Ji's single fold (0.941) matched or beat the five-fold average (0.939). That fits our saturated OOF: more folds of the same recipe add little, so the budget is better spent on different recipes. Students can reuse the existing teacher OOF soft labels, so a full-data refit needs no OOF of its own; the cost is that such members cannot be weighted or validated individually, so a fold-level A/B must justify each recipe first.

**Resolution and augmentation are the two measurable cheap knobs.** Going from 224 to 288 px gave +0.006 OOF with 11 of 12 labels up, led by lateral meniscus (+0.010) and fracture (+0.008) ([EXPERIMENTS.md](D:/kaggle/rsna-knee/EXPERIMENTS.md)), and the RSNA 2025 winners found the same direction at larger scale: +0.014 for a larger crop resolution ([1st place](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/rsna-intracranial-aneurysm-detection/writeups/1st-place-solution)) and 0.77 to 0.81 LB for a stride change that enlarged feature maps, confounded with an input-size change ([3rd place](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/rsna-intracranial-aneurysm-detection/writeups/3rd-place-solution)). Against that, forum opinion puts the sweet spot at 224-288 px ([tennogh and others](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/rsna-knee-abnormality-detection/discussion/735304#3520511)) and RadImageNet lost ground at 288 in our test, although public-stack members run at 336-384 px ([rsna-versia-5](https://www.kaggle.com/code/evgendvorkin/rsna-versia-5)). The existing 320 px cache supports img 320 for EfficientNet at no prep cost; anything higher needs a new CPU prep, which is free. For augmentation, our `to_input` rotates by only +/-10 degrees, and a +/-25 degree rotation was worth +0.025 CV in the 2025 4th-place cumulative table ([4th place](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/rsna-intracranial-aneurysm-detection/writeups/4th-place-solution)); the counter-evidence is that the 2022 runner-up found heavier augmentation no better ([2nd place](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/rsna-2022-cervical-spine-fracture-detection/writeups/rawe-2nd-place-solution-segmentation-2-5d-cnn-gru-)), so this is a 0.7 GPU-hour A/B, not a bet. Feeding per-token attention logits into the study loss as a slice-level auxiliary supervision signal appears in the 1st, 3rd and 4th lumbar write-ups ([1st place](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/rsna-2024-lumbar-spine-degenerative-classification/writeups/avengers-1st-place-solution), [3rd place](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/rsna-2024-lumbar-spine-degenerative-classification/writeups/sonyspine-s-tkmn-moyashii-3rd-place-solution)) but without isolated numbers, and the only measured auxiliary gains used real segmentation masks.

**Insurance costs no GPU and protects against the largest losses seen in this competition.** A bf16 autocast on T4 turned a 0.851 notebook into 0.710 by blowing the time guard ([thread 744230](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/rsna-knee-abnormality-detection/discussion/744230#3529827)); we already mandate fp16 and our blend notebooks fall back to the public file on failure ([CLAUDE.md](D:/kaggle/rsna-knee/CLAUDE.md)), but an own-only final submission should get the same fallback, in the spirit of the 2025 winner's output of per-class OOF means when preprocessing fails ([1st place](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/rsna-intracranial-aneurysm-detection/writeups/1st-place-solution)). The slice-order trap that cost another competitor 0.0276 on one fold (at a much lower AUC level) does not apply, because `knee.py` already sorts by ImagePositionPatient ([thread 745759](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/rsna-knee-abnormality-detection/discussion/745759#3532620)). The host has not said whether every hidden study has all three planes ([thread 744519](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/rsna-knee-abnormality-detection/discussion/744519#3531214)); slot dropout is the existing hedge, and in 2025 a model that estimated voxel spacing from image appearance when DICOM tags were missing lifted private from 0.84 to 0.85 ([3rd place](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/rsna-intracranial-aneurysm-detection/writeups/3rd-place-solution)). The efficiency prize stays out of reach: 0.01 AUC is worth about 11.7 minutes of runtime, so a 0.015 deficit to the efficiency leaders cannot be repaid with speed ([Efficiency Prize Evaluation](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/rsna-knee-abnormality-detection/overview/efficiency-prize-evaluation)).

## Foundation models disappoint once VRAM, rules and the public stack are counted

The 2024-2026 foundation-model wave offers one encoder with knee-MRI pretraining and a handful of general or multi-region models, and none has independent evidence on a RSNA-style twelve-finding task. The most direct knee result is the Medical Slice Transformer, where a fine-tuned DINOv2-S reached AUC 0.85 on MRNet meniscus against 0.78 for an ImageNet ResNet-50 slice encoder (P=0.07), a frozen DINOv2 fell to 0.79, and DINOv2-B did not beat DINOv2-S ([MST](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.15802)). That supports fine-tuning a small ViT but not a foundation-model jump. In our competition a DINOv2-S to DINOv2-B swap moved CV by only +0.0011 against a 0.0020 noise floor ([stevenleehans](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/rsna-knee-abnormality-detection/discussion/735154#3512730)), and the public stack already carries about 20 DINOv2-S members ([rsna-versia-5](https://www.kaggle.com/code/evgendvorkin/rsna-versia-5)), so a DINO member from us would be highly correlated with what we blend into. Our own DINOv2-S run underfit (0.847 versus 0.889 OOF on folds 0-1), and the published recipes that fix underfitting train to plateau (AnyMC3D runs up to 100 epochs with LoRA) ([AnyMC3D](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.12887)); at 65-70 minutes per six-epoch fold, that is out of budget.

## Label-side tricks and other ideas stay speculative or already spent

Several well-argued ideas cannot be validated with the measurement tools we have, so they sit below the measurable levers even where the literature is favourable. The strongest is the NVUM recipe: a per-sample moving average of the model's own logits as a regularisation target plus a log class-prior shift, which beat plain BCE by 3 to 5 AUC points on chest X-ray with clean radiologist test sets while ELR and co-teaching were indistinguishable from BCE ([NVUM](https://arxiv.org/abs/2103.04053)). It is the same family as our soft bootstrapping, which already captures the cross-architecture version of that gain, and the OOF-versus-report-label metric cannot show whether stacking the two helps. Site-balanced sampling was the most effective fix in a controlled shortcut study ([Muller et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.18792)) and the PANDA winners cleaned labels differently per data provider ([repo](https://github.com/kentaroy47/kaggle-panda-1st-place-solution)), but no study tests site-dependent report bias with a clean multi-site MRI test set. A heavier pseudo-label weight of 0.65-0.7 has one supporter and one contradicting report ([Archit Konde](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/rsna-knee-abnormality-detection/discussion/735304#3525193), [Nicolai Karcher](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/rsna-knee-abnormality-detection/discussion/735304#3529062)). OOF-disagreement filtering gave the lumbar runner-up +1% but failed to replicate for a third party ([2nd place](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/rsna-2024-lumbar-spine-degenerative-classification/writeups/ianpan-kevin-yuji-bartley-2nd-place-solution), [summary](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/rsna-2024-lumbar-spine-degenerative-classification/discussion/541279)), and soft bootstrapping is the soft form of it. If any of these gets a slot, it should ride on a single calibration submission, not an OOF comparison.

The remaining ideas fail on cost or on negative evidence. Two-stage localisation by TotalSegmentator MRI, attention-derived ROIs or self-annotated boxes has no RSNA precedent without labels, and a Slicer-forum thread says TotalSegmentator's appendicular-bone module has no knee soft-tissue classes ([Slicer forum](https://discourse.slicer.org/t/how-to-segment-the-tibia-using-totalsegmentator)). Co-teaching and DivideMix are at or below plain cross-entropy on real report noise and hurt under class imbalance ([LNMBench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.09315)); group DRO and adversarial site training did not beat ERM in a ten-dataset medical benchmark ([MEDFAIR](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.01725)). Bi-GRU or transformer heads over slice features are contradictory in the winners' write-ups (the 1st-place lumbar solution preferred bi-LSTM, the 3rd found sequence heads unhelpful), and our MIL head already carries slice-position embeddings. CutMix has one unnumbered lumbar claim and an awkward label-mixing meaning for localised findings. Bigger encoders, long schedules, focal loss, TTA and extra pseudo-label rounds appear on the winners' negative lists and on ours ([EXPERIMENTS.md](D:/kaggle/rsna-knee/EXPERIMENTS.md)).

Final selection should hedge rather than chase the public board. The test set is about 1,300 studies ([Data page](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/rsna-knee-abnormality-detection/data)), a Grandmaster calls the public 0.945 notebook an overfit blend and expects teams at 0.935 or better that avoid it to move into silver ([thread 742327](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/rsna-knee-abnormality-detection/discussion/742327#3530018)), and a study of 11 medal competitions found teams just inside the bronze line medalled 24% of the time, the same as teams just outside it ([thread 742327](https://www.kaggle.com/competitions/rsna-knee-abnormality-detection/discussion/742327#3529510)). Pick one submission as the public-stack-plus-own blend and the other as a more own-weighted diverse blend that includes the partner's arm, rather than two near-identical blends.

## Conclusion

The research changes the question. Our attention-MIL design matches what the 2024-2025 winners and a 2025 benchmark converge on, so the remaining headroom sits in diversity, resolution and the people we can combine with, not in architecture or in pretrained weights. The most valuable external resource turns out to be a partner, not a checkpoint: the foundation models that look most relevant (OrthoFoundation, DINOv3) are either an eligibility gamble or no better than what the public stack already contains, and the winners' largest levers were annotations we cannot obtain.

Two things would change this plan. A knee-blend-288 score of 0.946 or higher would justify keeping a heavy own-model weight and spending the October 17-20 quota on members; a host ruling on OAI or fastMRI weights would reopen OrthoFoundation, although its ViT-L size and the frozen-feature collapse seen in MST would still make a T4 fine-tune impractical. Unresolved at the time of writing: the notes did not open the RSNA 2025 runner-up's paper or the host's individual rulings in topics 734109, 735497, 737950 and 738111, the hidden-test plane composition is unknown, and the recipe behind the 0.955-0.963 teams remains unpublished, which is why top-10 stays a long shot.
