## The one thing: we wrote off our own models on evidence that could not detect anything
- We stopped using them after two add-ons at **weight 0.05** to base958 both read 0.957 against base958's 0.958.
  - At 5 % weight the expected effect is a few 1e-4: smaller than the 0.001 rounding step, and far smaller than the public split's SD of 0.003.
  - Both results are consistent with a tiny loss, with no effect, or with a tiny gain sitting just under a rounding boundary.
- Our best own set now is zoom + cnx288ptl + cnxz: rank-average OOF **0.9078** against report labels, **0.934** on gold-58.
  - That is the level of the best public single models: the nartaa S6 ConvNeXt has fold-0 OOF 0.907 and gold 0.931 and scored **LB 0.950** on its own; nartaa's CoAtNet has gold 0.919 and scored LB 0.949.
- Caveat: our 10-04 ensemble mapped worse (gold 0.923 → LB 0.938). Projected from the +0.011 OOF gain since then, our set lands around **0.945–0.950**. **This has never been measured.**
- What the public frontier shows, measured: "a strong, *different* second model" is what moves a blend.
  - S6 + Raptor 30/70: 0.949 → 0.954. Two ~0.95 families at 50/50: 0.956.
  - Our models are trained differently from every public family: our own pseudo-label recipe, 31-slice canonical-left MIL, and EfficientNet/ConvNeXt-T at 288 and on the 110 mm zoom.
- So the untested hypothesis with the most upside: **ours as a real third family (weight 0.2–0.3), not a 5 % add-on.**

## How to test it cheaply (the rank inside a block works as a paired test)
- The union and the variant with ours share most of their predictions, so the difference between their public scores is a paired comparison with far less noise than 0.003.
- Our team rank moves only if the new submission beats our previous best. A rank jump therefore says the change helped.
